Conversation starters
Ways to start the talk

Open the door

Questions with no right answer.

  • "How are you feeling about this interview?"
  • "What part are you looking forward to?"
  • "What's on your mind about it?"

Hand them the choice

They decide how much help they get.

  • "Do you want help with this, or do you want to handle it yourself?"
  • "Would it help more if I listened or gave ideas?"
  • "Is now a good time to talk about it, or later?"

Listen without fixing

Use these after they share.

  • "That makes sense."
  • "What do you think you'll do?"
  • "Do you want my thoughts, or did you just need to say it out loud?"

Repair if you overstep

Everyone jumps in too fast sometimes. These keep trust strong.

  • "I think I jumped in too fast. Let me try again."
  • "You've got this one. I'll step back."
  • "Tell me if I'm helping too much."
How to ask.
  • Talk side by side. A car ride, a walk or cooking together often feels easier than sitting face to face.
  • Ask one question at a time. Give them time to answer.
  • Skip "why" questions. They can feel like a test. Try "what" or "how."
  • Let them say no. "Not now" is a real answer.
Go first
Share a little of your own story

When you open up first, it's easier for them to open up too. Keep it short and bring it back to them.

"My first interview made me so nervous I forgot the interviewer's name. What feels hard for you?"

The support ladder

Each step on your roadmap has a level. Start at the lightest level that works. Next time, try one level lighter.
